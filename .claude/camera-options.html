IP cameras for the door

Get a wired PoE camera (power and video over one network cable) that supports RTSP and ONVIF and is set up from a web browser. The app reads it directly, so nothing from the camera's maker has to run. An ESP32-CAM isn't a good door camera; keep the ESP32 for the lock relay.

Prices checked on Mercado Livre on 24 October 2026, when all of these were in stock. Prices change often.

What matters for this app

RTSP and ONVIF, set up in a browser

Add the camera in Cameras, or with Find cameras on this network. Avoid cameras whose listing only mentions a phone app (Yoosee, iCSee, V380, Tuya/Smart Life, Xiaomi). Many have no RTSP, and on the rest it's unreliable.

Cable beats Wi-Fi

One cable carries power and video for up to 100 m, which suits a server far from the door. Wi-Fi works, but it drops frames.

Distance

The app ignores faces narrower than 80 px (min_face_px). With these wide lenses, people need to pass within about 1.5–2 m of the camera. Mount it at about head height near where people stop, not high in a corner.

Light

People arrive before sunrise. In the dark, cameras switch to black-and-white infrared, and recognition gets worse because the enrolled photos are in color. A lamp at the entrance helps more than extra megapixels. In daylight it flips: a camera facing a bright open doorway needs good backlight handling (WDR).

Power over the cable

The two cable cameras take their power over the network cable (PoE). At the server end you need a PoE switch, or a PoE injector following the 802.3af standard. A TP-Link TL-POE150S or TL-POE160S costs about R$ 140–260.

Mercado Livre search TL-POE150S

ESP32-CAM

Not for the door camera.

  • The problem isn't resolution; it's the sensor (OV2640). The image is noisy, it's poor in low light, and it handles backlight badly.
  • It only has 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi with a tiny antenna, and it's sensitive to its power supply.
  • It would likely plug into the app as it is: OpenCV can read its video stream (http://IP:81/stream in the standard example).
  • It isn't really cheaper: R$ 120 from an official store, against R$ 199 for the Tapo, which has a much better image.
  • The ESP32 is the right part for the door lock relay, as planned.
